# Chalk & Clock — a visual philosophy

**Movement:** Chalk & Clock (aligns with the interim PICKUP brand direction)
**Status:** design philosophy for the ManHunt motion workstream (`design/social/motion/`)
**Derived from:** `design/brand/strategy/brand-platform.md` §2 "PICKUP", §6.1 beat sheet, `docs/BRAND_BRIEF.md`, `docs/MAP_SAMPLES.md`, the app's dark-theme tokens, and the ManHunt-owned app renders.

---

A game that is played in borrowed places should look like it belongs to them. This is the first principle: the surface is asphalt, not glass. Deep navy is a flat field, not a gradient; marks are chalk, not light. Nothing glows, because nothing in a park at eight in the evening glows — the light comes from the street and from the phone in your pocket. The palette is therefore four decisions and no more: asphalt to hold the world, chalk to draw on it, an official cyan that belongs to the field and the referee and never to a team, and the two kit colours — coral for Prey, violet for Hunters. Every visual element must be one of those four things doing one of those four jobs. If it is not, it is decoration and it is removed.

Geometry is borrowed, not invented. Touchlines, corner arcs, a halfway line, a centre circle: these are the most recognisable markings in sport, and they cost nothing to draw. They become the game's language without illustration or metaphor — the centre circle is the play zone, the goal line is the edge of the search area, the pursuit line is a chalk stroke that happens to be running. The frame is deliberately sparse so that a single moving dot can carry an entire scene. That economy is not minimalism as a style; it is the only way a phone-sized image can hold a full game. Every line weight, every dash rhythm, every margin is set by hand and re-checked at 390 px wide, because the work is judged in a hand, not on a wall.

Type is a verbal system as much as a visual one. A condensed athletic grotesque does the shouting — captions, the cover slogan, a clock that reads like a stadium scoreboard. A plain sans carries the one sentence that needs to be read rather than felt. A monospaced face carries the numbers that must be trusted: the clock, the URL, the honest install line. These three registers never blur, and the mark itself is not type at all: it is the owner-approved drawn vector lockup, used as artwork and never retyped, letter-spaced or restyled. Text is sparse and always load-bearing: a caption is a sentence, never a paragraph; the clock is a number, never a claim. The discipline is strict because the voice is warm — plain words, no hype, no promised safety, no invented users. The work must feel meticulously typeset, each line optically corrected, as though a person at the top of the craft set it and then set it again.

Motion obeys two laws. The first is the clock: it is the brand's recurring visual, so it locks in with a physical snap around the fourth second and then simply counts — no bouncing, no drama, because a clock does not care how the game feels. The second is the mark: every transition is a field event. A chalk line sweeps, a circle is drawn, a trail is laid, a ring closes, a tag lands in two frames of coral. The edit cuts on those events, hard, with one single frame of black at the turn and one two-frame inversion at the tag. Nothing moves without a reason that could be explained to a referee.

The whole piece is judged by a simple test: if it were projected on the wall of a five-a-side pitch at night, would it look like it was made for that wall? The answer is yes only if it stays flat, stays honest, and stays legible in a hand — the first frame already says something, the last frame gives an address, and the twenty-five seconds in between feel like the first minute of a game. That is the standard: not effects, but a precise, hand-calibrated instrument for starting a pickup game tonight.

## Execution notes for this workstream

- **Palette lock:** asphalt `#0B1220`, flat block `#0E1727`, chalk `#E6EDF7`, official cyan `#22D3EE` (field/referee — never a team), Prey coral `#FF7A59`, Hunters violet `#A79BFF`. Flat fills only; no gradients, no glow (5 % monochrome grain is the only texture).
- **Type system:** Big Shoulders (condensed display: captions, cover slogan, clock), Instrument Sans (end-card slogan/CTA), Red Hat Mono (clock label, URL, install microcopy, team labels). The MANHUNT lockup is the owner-approved Open Zone vector (mark + 7-path drawn wordmark), inlined verbatim from `design/brand/identity/final/`; never retype, letter-space, recolour, outline or rotate it, and keep its baked-in clear space empty.
- **Clock:** tabular numerals, top-right, locked frame with chalk rules; starts at the host-set default `60:00`, never presented as fixed.
- **Safe area:** 84 px side margins, 320 px bottom exclusion for platform chrome; captions bottom edge at 1560 px.
- **Rule:** no real people, no stock photography, no testimonials, no safety promises, no "anywhere is yours" imagery. The pitch is borrowed and shared.
