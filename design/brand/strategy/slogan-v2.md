## 1. Autopsy: why the v1 lines failed

The old copy wasn't wrong about the product. It was wrong about the **job**. Every rejected line
describes *the system* to someone who has not asked about the system yet. Feed copy has to put the
viewer **inside a situation**.

| Line | What it does | Why it fails |
|---|---|---|
| `No gear. No fee. Just players.` | Removes three objections | **Three negations before any desire.** Nobody in a feed has an objection yet — the line answers a question that hasn't been asked. It's a spec sheet: price, equipment. And the only noun with a picture in it is "players". No verb, no "you", no friends, no chase, no tonight. It is a *why-us* block, not a slogan. |
| `your street has rules now` | Sets a mood | "Rules" is an institutional abstraction — it sounds like a council notice or a board game manual. The fun of the game is *disorder* the server merely arbitrates. No stake, no second person, no verb of action. It promises ownership of a street the game cannot give you. |
| `two teams` | States inventory | Zero tension. The map already shows two colours. Fills the most valuable beat in the film with a fact the picture has already delivered. |
| `one clock` | States a system property | A clock is what you *wait* for; in this game it's what you *run* from. The line drains the tension it should create. And it is not a human stake — nobody cares that there is one clock. |
| `the map already knows` | Tries curiosity | The best of the six, but "knows" is soft and "already" implies backstory the viewer doesn't have. Its payoff was a screenshot the owner (rightly) dislikes: the reveal landed on a picture, not on an idea. |
| `tagged` | Pays off the tag | Fine as a *beat*, useless as *content*: genre-neutral, and in social English "tagged" means a photo mention. It only works when the visual explains it. |
| `No gear. No fee. Just players.` (end card) | Conversion slot | A conversion card needs a **verb**, not a benefit. It was a positioning statement in a CTA's clothing. |

**The diagnosis in one sentence:** v1 wrote like a brand book (properties, system, negativity) for a
surface that needs a situation (stakes, second person, verbs).

## 2. The rules the new copy is built on

1. **Second person in the first two words** — "your", "you", "bet".
2. **Present tense, one verb per line.** Something is happening *now*.
3. **Stakes, not properties.** What happens to *you*, not how the system works.
4. **≤ 5 words per beat.** 7 only for the single "explains it" beat. If it needs a breath, it's too long.
5. **No negation before a positive.** Say what it is, not what it costs.
6. **Every line must survive as a text-only caption.** If it only works as a voiceover, it's not social-native.
7. **End card = a verb.** A thing the viewer can do in the next ten seconds.
8. **No fixed hunt length, no safety promise, no fake urgency.** (Unchanged guardrails: `tone-of-voice.md` §4.)

## 3. Candidates

Five families, deliberately different in *strategy*, not just wording. `ch` = characters;
`360` = estimated one-line width at 13 sp against 320 dp usable (method in `slogans.md` §2);
`spoken` = syllables and the read-aloud verdict; `hook` = scroll-stop potential out of 5 with the
app unseen.

## 4. Top 3 and the recommendation

| Rank | Line | Job | Why it wins | Risk / mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Your friends are hunting you.** | Flagship slogan + 15 s hook | Highest scroll-stop of anything tested here: five words, no jargon, no app knowledge, and it makes a viewer think about *their own* group chat in under a second. It is a situation, not a claim. | Reads menacing on a trust-critical page → use the short form there; video/feed only otherwise |
| **2** | **Hide from your friends.** | Permanent slogan: app tagline, install page `<h1>`, bio, icon lockup | Same family, threat removed, 4 syllables, fits 360 dp even at 2× text scale, and it is literally the game. Safe next to an APK download warning. | Slightly "antisocial" out of context → always paired with the descriptor line "real-world hide-and-seek" |
| **3** | **Bet you can't hide.** | Alternate hook / A-B test | Pure challenge. Drives comments ("I'd win"), doubles as a rewatch hook, works muted. | Explains nothing — only usable on a surface where the map is already explaining |

**Recommended pair (one idea, two jobs):**
- **Flagship / hook:** `Your friends are hunting you.`
- **Short form (360 dp, trust-critical, lockups):** `Hide from your friends.`
- **End card / caption CTA:** `Create a hunt. Send the code. Hide.`

The short form is not a second slogan — it is the same line with the threat dialled down, so the two
can never contradict each other. If the owner wants exactly one line everywhere, **"Hide from your
friends."** is the single safe choice; if the short is the hero asset, lead it with
**"Your friends are hunting you."**

## 5. Spoken-word test

Protocol used: read each line aloud three times at natural pace with a phone stopwatch; count
syllables, mark where the stress lands, and check that no consonant cluster has to be forced.

| Line | Syllables | Read time (natural) | Verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Your friends are hunting you. | 6 | ~1.1 s | Clean. Stress on HUNT; the "s" of friends flows into "are". Sayable with a smirk or a deadpan — that range is why it's the hook |
| Hide from your friends. | 4 | ~0.7 s | Easiest in the set; two clear beats. No breath needed |
| Bet you can't hide. | 4 | ~0.8 s | Clicks on the /t/ of "can't"; the stress lands on BET, which is where the dare lives |
| Your phone is a snitch. | 5 | ~0.9 s | Two /f/, /s/ neighbours but no collision; SNITCH is the payoff word |
| Your phone tells them where you are. | 8 | ~1.4 s | Fine as VO, too long for a 1.5 s hook |
| Create a hunt. Send the code. Hide. | 8 | ~2.0 s | Three commands, three breaths — reads like instructions, which is the point |

Rule for the edit: **if a recorded VO line needs a breath mid-line, the line is cut, not sped up.**

## 6. Arabic adaptations

Register rule unchanged: **MSA is the in-app default; Egyptian colloquial is for social and hooks.**
Do not mix registers in one asset. Match the app's existing vocabulary — `مطاردة` for a match,
`الفريسة`/`الصيادون` for roles, `الوسم` for the tag.

| English | MSA | Egyptian (social) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Your friends are hunting you.** | **أصدقاؤك يطاردونك.** | **أصحابك بيطاردوك.** | Best hook in Arabic too — 8 syllables, stress on يطاردوك. `يطارد` (chase) is warmer and less predatory than `يصطاد` (hunt); prefer it in copy even though the UI keeps الصيادون |
| **Hide from your friends.** | **اختبئ من أصدقائك.** | **استخبى من أصحابك.** | Short, idiomatic, fits one line at 360 dp with room to spare |
| Your phone is a snitch. | **هاتفك يفضحك.** | **موبايلك يفضحك.** | `يفضحك` ("exposes you") is stronger in Arabic than a literal "snitch"; it keeps the joke and adds sting |
| Bet you can't hide. | **أتحداك أن تختبئ.** | **أتحداك تستخبى.** | The dare construction is native to both registers; Egyptian is punchier for video |
| Your phone tells them where you are. | **هاتفك يخبرهم أين أنت.** | **موبايلك بيقولهم إنت فين.** | Plain and accurate |
| Move. Or get tagged. | **تحرّك، أو يوسمونك.** | **اتحرك، أو هيوسموك.** | `يوسمونك` matches the app's tag verb exactly |
| Tagged. Round over. | **تم وسمك. انتهت الجولة.** | **اتوسمت. خلصت الجولة.** | Two short sentences; do not merge them |
| Create a hunt. Send the code. Hide. | **أنشئ مطاردة. أرسل الرمز. اختبئ.** | **اعمل مطاردة. ابعت الكود. استخبى.** | Three commands map 1:1; `الرمز` is the code, `الكود` in colloquial |
| Hide and seek, but it's real. | **استغماية، لكنها حقيقية.** | **استغماية، بس حقيقية.** | Egyptian `استغماية` is the playground word — use it in social, never in UI |

Arabic checks before shipping: one line at 360 dp at 1.5× text scale; timer digits stay LTR; no
Latin inside an Arabic sentence; no transliteration of the slogan; never mix MSA and Egyptian in one
asset; re-measure because Arabic joined script is wider than the Latin estimate.

## 8. Trademark / generic screen

Exact-phrase sweep at time of writing found **no identical commercial claim** for the top three. The
closest adjacent territory is game copy in the same conceit — e.g. *What Lurks Below* uses "Run from
your friends. Then hunt them." ([shuri.gg](https://shuri.gg/games/100252674-what-lurks-below)) and a
Steam co-op title ships as *Your Friends Vs Hidebehind*
([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4634660/Your_Friends_Vs_Hidebehind/)). That is a
useful signal, not a problem: the idea is legible enough that other games reach for it, and these
lines are plain-language sentences that would be refused registration anyway — they only need not to
collide. They don't.

This is a screen, not legal advice. `MANHUNT` itself remains the bigger question
(`brand-platform.md` §10, D1) and is unchanged by this document.
