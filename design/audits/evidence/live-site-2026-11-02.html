01The field

The map is the field.

Hunters see the latest report, not a perfect live feed. Prey never see the map. The zone and the trails are the host's call — the server keeps the clock.

Prey

Hide. Stay free.

Your phone reports your position to the Hunters at the room's interval. Stay free until the clock stops — if one Prey is still out, Prey win.

Hunters

Track. Tag.

Follow the latest reports and trails. Find a Prey and tag them in person — they confirm or dispute it, and the server decides what counts.

04The rules

The pitch is borrowed. The rules are not.

The chase is exciting. The rules are boring, on purpose. The parts the server owns are not up for argument.

Clock

The clock is server-owned.

Timers, phases and pause/resume are decided server-side. No phone can run the clock ahead.

Teams

Two sides, counted.

Prey and Hunters are assigned in the room and enforced by the server for the whole round.

Tags

A tag needs a witness.

A Hunter claims a tag; the Prey confirms or disputes it. Duplicate claims cannot double-apply.

Map

Latest report only.

Hunters see the last report and optional trails — not a perfect live feed. Prey never see positions.

Signal

Built to recover.

Reconnects, restarts and flaky signal do not break the round. Pause and resume shift the clock correctly.

Hosting

Yours to host.

One Node process, one SQLite file, no Docker, no paid map or push service. Read the deployment guide.

Language

English and Arabic.

Full RTL, dark-first UI, adjustable text size and 48 dp controls throughout the app.

Cost

Free, no ads.

MIT-licensed source. No ads, no in-app purchases, no account email required.

Privacy and safety

  • Your position is shown only to the Hunters in your room while a match is running. Admins get no extra location access.
  • Location reports are sent by a foreground service with a visible notification. History is shown to Hunters only when the host turns trails on.
  • There is no email address and no password recovery — keep your password safe.
  • The hosted server is a test deployment on plain HTTP. Don't use it for sensitive location data; self-host behind HTTPS for that.
  • ManHunt is a game, not a safety service. Play in places you're allowed to be, with people you trust.

07The questions

The honest answers.

What the page can't show you: the small print, said plainly. Nothing here is a promise the project can't keep.

Is ManHunt free?

Yes. The APK is free to download and the server is MIT-licensed. You can play on the hosted test server or run the backend yourself. There are no ads and no in-app purchases.

Why is it not on Google Play?

ManHunt is distributed as a signed, sideloaded APK. That means allowing installs from your browser and possibly seeing a Play Protect warning. The release and its SHA-256 are published on GitHub — the same hash is printed on this page.

How many phones do we need?

At least two, and everyone should be in the same physical area. One phone hosts the room and shares the 6-character code; the others join and pick a side. Groups of four to ten are where the game works best.

Who can see my location?

Only the Hunters in your room, during the hunting phase, see your latest report. Prey never see locations, and admins get no extra location access. Location history is shown only when the host enables trails.

Is the hosted server safe for a real game?

It is a plain-HTTP test server with no availability guarantee and no account recovery. Use it for test games; for anything sensitive, self-host behind HTTPS and play with people you trust.

What if a tag is disputed?

The Hunter claims the tag, the Prey confirms or disputes it, and the Huntmaster resolves it. Duplicate claims cannot double-apply, so the result can't be argued into existence.
