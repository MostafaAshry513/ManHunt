LAUNCHER ICON STUDY - 02
CHARACTER + OBJECT / NIGHT SHIFT
NOT APPLIED YET

Round one was eight marks. None of them had a player in it. This round turns the icon back into a game: a hider, a hound, a searchlight, a runner, a patch, an eye, an M, a claw. Same palette and signature, none of the geometry repeated.

PRODUCT
A live two-team hide-and-seek game played on real streets.
CHARACTER
Alive, tense, a little dangerous. A game people play, not a dashboard they use.
WORLD
Night streets, a wall to hide behind, a torch, a crew patch.
MOMENT
The second before you are found, or the second before you find someone.
SIGNATURE
One lime report point on every direction.

Why round one failed

  • All eight were marks. Ticket, ring, fold, grid, monogram - no player, no story, no reason to pick one up.
  • Too polite. The palette was right; the forms were all thin, centred, logo-exercise geometry.
  • Same silhouette family. At a glance most of them read as a map or a scanner, not a hunt.
  • No character. The app pages have a voice; the icons had a grid.

What this round changes

  • Big single forms. A head, a hound, a beam, a body. Fewer elements, bigger shapes, less line work.
  • Game-first reads. Hide, hunt, search, run; the product's verbs before its system diagrams.
  • One point of life. Every direction still keeps the lime report point.
  • Tested at launcher size. 48 / 32 / 24 px, circle and squircle, monochrome.
